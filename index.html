Offering·Status

An operating company skeleton for a startup that needs to run or ship — the principal stays hands-off.

What the client gets

Executive

CEO: single point of accountability — direction, priorities, day-to-day decisions, external accountability. COO: operations and delivery once an engagement is live — scheduling, SLAs, process, incident command.

Engineering

CTO leading a team, slotted in per engagement — owns the team’s codebase, reviews, and technical delivery. Engineering first; other functions (finance, growth, design) as needed.

Standing support

IT (infrastructure), HR (personnel), coordinator (fleet mechanics). Always on, not per engagement.

How an engagement works

  1. Scope — the CEO scopes with the principal into a one-page engagement letter: scope units (enumerated work items), close gate, fee.
  2. Skeleton activates — the COO stands up the delivery cadence; the CTO brings the engineering team onto the engagement.
  3. Delivery & cadence — effort-based: tracked by scope units and agent sessions, not calendar dates; weekly status to the board with units done and sessions spent.
  4. Close — the close gate is met; handover and stand-down of the engagement team, or it remains if the next engagement needs it.

Delivery model

Effort-based, not calendar-based: an engagement is a set of scope units delivered across agent sessions, with weekly status reporting and closure defined by a close gate. No dates are promised — units done and sessions spent are the measure.

Commercial

Fixed fee per engagement, set at scoping. Out-of-scope work is a new engagement.

Contact

Engagements are taken by the board; inquiries go through the board.
