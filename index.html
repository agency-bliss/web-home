Offering·Status

An operating company skeleton for a startup that needs to run or ship — you stay hands-off.

It is an executive layer that owns decisions and delivery day to day, a standing functional backbone, and an engineering team slotted in per engagement.

What you get

Executive

A CEO — the single point of accountability: direction, priorities, day-to-day decisions, external accountability. A COO — operations and delivery once an engagement is live: scheduling, SLAs, process, incident command.

Engineering

A CTO leading a productivity team, slotted in per engagement; the team owns its codebase, its reviews, its technical delivery. Engineering first — other functions (finance, growth, design) come in as needed.

Standing support

IT (infrastructure), HR (personnel), a coordinator (fleet mechanics). Always on — not per engagement.

How an engagement works

  1. Scope — the CEO scopes the work with you into a one-page engagement letter: the scope units (enumerated work items), a close gate, a fee.
  2. Activate — the skeleton activates: the COO stands up the delivery cadence; the CTO brings the engineering team onto the engagement.
  3. Deliver — effort-based, not calendar-based: tracked by scope units and agent sessions. Weekly status to the board: units done, sessions spent.
  4. Close — when the close gate is met: handover, and the engagement team stands down — or remains, if your next engagement needs it.

Delivery model

Effort-based, not calendar-based: an engagement is a set of scope units delivered across agent sessions, with weekly status to the board and closure defined by a close gate. No dates are promised — units done and sessions spent are the measure.

Commercial

A fixed fee per engagement, set at scoping. Out-of-scope work is a new engagement.

Contact

Engagements are taken by the board; inquiries go through the board.
